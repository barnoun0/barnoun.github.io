Understanding VLANs: a practical overview

VLANs (Virtual LANs) are one of those networking topics that sound abstract until you actually need them. This post is my raw notes — what I wish someone told me before I started slicing broadcast domains.

What problem do VLANs solve?

A flat L2 network has two major issues: broadcast storms and security. If one device sends a broadcast, every device sees it. VLANs allow you to split a physical switch into multiple isolated logical switches.

VLAN tagging (802.1Q)

When a frame travels between switches, it needs a tag (4-byte header) to know which VLAN it belongs to. The most common:

VLAN 1   - default (avoid using)
VLAN 10  - engineering
VLAN 20  - HR
VLAN 99  - native (untagged)

Access vs. Trunk ports

  • Access port — belongs to exactly one VLAN (e.g., a PC)
  • Trunk port — carries multiple VLANs (switch-to-switch)

Real-world config (Cisco-style)

interface GigabitEthernet0/1
 switchport mode access
 switchport access vlan 10

interface GigabitEthernet0/24
 switchport mode trunk
 switchport trunk allowed vlan 10,20,99

Why I use VLANs in my homelab

- Separate IoT devices (untrusted)
- Management VLAN for switches & iLO/iDRAC
- Guest WiFi isolation

Takeaway: VLANs are not encryption — they're convenience + isolation. Use ACLs or firewalls for real segmentation.
